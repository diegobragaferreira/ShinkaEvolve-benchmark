The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

However, introducing an adaptive learning rate—such as decaying the step size based on iteration count or convergence progress—can improve both speed and final score. Adaptive steps allow more aggressive moves early in optimization when the landscape is rough, followed by fine-tuning as the solution approaches optimality, mimicking approaches seen in successful evolutionary and optimization frameworks.

2. **Add elite preservation to the sequence evolution mechanism to prevent loss of good solutions**  
   Drawing from successful evolutionary strategies found in "Evolutionary Sequence Optimizer" (Generation 5), introduce a mechanism to preserve high-performing sequences across iterations. For example, retain the top 10% of sequences from each generation based on their evaluated performance before applying the new direction update. This prevents regression caused by random perturbations and allows the population to evolve toward better optima, particularly useful in noisy or complex landscapes where premature convergence may occur.

3. **Integrate multiple fallback strategies when LP solving fails**  
   The current implementation has a single fallback for when `g_fun` is None, but further robustness can be achieved by expanding this into a multi-tiered fallback strategy. When LP fails, attempt alternative initialization techniques (e.g., exponential decay or mirrored sequence patterns), or apply small random perturbations with bounded magnitude to ensure continued evolution. 4. **Use a warm-start approach for LP solver initialization to improve convergence stability**  
   Instead of solving the LP from scratch every time, initialize the LP solver with the last known optimal solution (or its closest approximation) as a starting point. This leverages the principle observed in programs like “Autocorrelation Sequence Optimizer,” where stable initializations help maintain numerical accuracy and avoid local minima. Warm-starting leads to faster convergence and reduces chances of failure in repeated LP calls, especially important when updating directions iteratively in sequence optimization tasks.

5. **Apply FFT-based convolution in the LP constraint formulation for scalability and efficiency**  
   While the current program computes convolutions directly via loops, using FFT-based convolution in constructing the LP constraints would align with the top-performing method seen in programs like "Convolution-based Sequence Optimization." This change enables tighter control over convolution bounds and supports larger-scale problems without sacrificing runtime or solution quality, directly building on the evidence of FFT efficiency in prior successes.
