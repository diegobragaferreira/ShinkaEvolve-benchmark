<DESCRIPTION>
The current implementation uses uniform random placement for peaks, which can lead to clustering and suboptimal autoconvolution properties. I'm implementing a smarter peak spacing strategy that uses logarithmic distribution to spread peaks more evenly across the domain while maintaining good autoconvolution characteristics. This approach follows the successful pattern seen in previous implementations that beat AlphaEvolve.

The key insight is to use logarithmic spacing combined with adaptive peak density to avoid clustering while ensuring sufficient peak coverage. This should improve the likelihood of finding high-C2 solutions by creating more diverse and well-distributed peak structures.
