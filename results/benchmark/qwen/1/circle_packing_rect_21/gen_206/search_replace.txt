<DESCRIPTION>
I'll implement a Voronoi-based adaptive mutation strategy that weights mutation steps based on local constraint density. This approach will make the optimization more intelligent by applying smaller mutation steps in densely packed regions where constraints are tight, and larger steps in sparser regions where there's more freedom to adjust.

The key insight is that in regions with high constraint density (many neighboring circles), small mutations are safer and more effective for fine-tuning. In contrast, in low-density regions, larger mutations can explore more effectively and potentially discover better configurations.

This will enhance the existing `mutate_circles_adaptive` method to use Voronoi diagrams for computing constraint density more accurately, leading to better convergence behavior and potentially higher sum_radii values.

    def mutate_circles_adaptive(self, circles: np.ndarray, constraint_densities: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
        """Mutate circle positions and radii with adaptive strategy based on local constraints."""
        mutated = circles.copy()

        # Adaptive mutation parameters based on constraint density
        for i in range(self.n_circles):
            x, y, r = mutated[i]

            # Higher constraint density (more neighbors) = more careful mutation
            # Lower constraint density (fewer neighbors) = bolder mutation
            density_weight = 1.0 + constraint_densities[i] * 2.0  # Range 1.0 to 3.0

            pos_mutation_strength = 0.02 / density_weight
            rad_mutation_strength = 0.01 / density_weight
